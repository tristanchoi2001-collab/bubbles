## A. Adiabatic two-phase flow in narrow rectangular gaps
- [V] Mishima K, Hibiki T, Nishihara H (1993) Some characteristics of gas-liquid flow in narrow rectangular ducts. IJMF 19(1):115-124. DOI 10.1016/0301-9322(93)90027-R (DOI seen in earlier search result list). Vertical, 40 mm wide, 200 cm long, gaps 1.07/2.45/5.0 mm (other reviews: 1.0/2.4/5.0), neutron radiography void; bubbly/slug/churn/annular; no churn at 1 mm (per Xu 1999 review). C0 reported ~1.35 by one source vs 1.0-1.2 in OSTI record (conflict, check original). Very close to our 1/3/7 mm.
- [V] Xu JL, Cheng P, Zhao TS (1999) Gas-liquid two-phase flow regimes in rectangular channels with mini/micro gaps. IJMF (S0301932298000573). Gaps 0.3/0.6/1.0 mm. (authors to re-check)
- [V] Hibiki T, Mishima K (2001) Flow regime transition criteria for upward two-phase flow in vertical narrow rectangular channels. Nucl Eng Des 203 (S002954930000306X).
- [V] Sadatomi M, Sato Y, Saruwatari S (1982) Two-phase flow in vertical noncircular channels. IJMF (0301932282900684).
- [V] Chalgeri VS, Jeong JH (2019) Flow regime identification and classification based on void fraction and differential pressure of vertical two-phase flow in rectangular channel. IJHMT 132:802-816 (S0017931018344016). Pusan Natl Univ.

## B. Boiling in narrow gaps
- [V] Kim YH, Kim SJ, Kim JJ, Noh SW, Suh KY, Rempe JL, Cheung FB, Kim SB (2005) Visualization of boiling phenomena in inclined rectangular gap. IJMF 31(5):618-642. Saturated water 1 atm, gaps 1/2/5/10 mm, 90-180 deg (downward-facing). CHF falls with inclination and narrower gap, except downward-facing where CHF rises as gap narrows; transition angle.
- [V] Rempe JL et al. (2006) Internal vessel cooling feasibility attributed by critical heat flux in inclined rectangular gap. Nucl Technol 154(1):13-40.

## C. Pressure drop
- [V] Lee HJ, Lee SY (2001) Pressure drop correlations for two-phase flow within horizontal rectangular channels with small heights. IJMF 27(5):783-796. Air-water, gap 0.4-4 mm, width 20 mm, 305 points, L-M type with C depending on mass flux & gap, +/-10%, X 0.303-79.4, Re_Lo 175-17700.
- [V] Wilmarth T, Ishii M (1994) Two-phase flow regimes in narrow rectangular vertical and horizontal channels. IJHMT 37(12) (pages not seen). Air-water, gaps 1 & 2 mm, vertical + horizontal, CCD video. Horizontal: stratified smooth, plug, slug, dispersed bubbly, wavy annular. A 2018 KNS paper (Chalgeri) found Wilmarth map agrees best with its vertical upward data.
- [V] Ali MI, Sadatomi M, Kawaji M (1993) Adiabatic two-phase flow in narrow channels between two flat plates. Can J Chem Eng 71(5):657-666. Air-water, 240 mm long, gaps 0.778 & 1.465 mm, six orientations. Gap & orientation effects on pattern, void, friction small EXCEPT horizontal flow between vertical plates; void & friction multiplier correlated with Martinelli X.
- [P] Xu JL et al. (1999) Experimental study on gas-liquid two-phase flow regimes in rectangular channels with mini gaps. IJHFF (S0142727X99000028). Transition lines shift to higher j_g as gap increases.
- [P] Sadatomi 1982: per a search summary, rectangular channels with gaps ~7-17 mm; geometry had little impact on transitions (check).
- [P] Prog Nucl Energy 2021 (S0149197021001177) Air-water two-phase flow regime and transition criteria in vertical upward narrow rectangular channels; 1 mm gap side walls raise coalescence probability.
- [P] Prog Nucl Energy 2022 (S014919702200436X) Experimental study on flow patterns in narrow rectangular channels.
- [P] Prog Nucl Energy 2020 (S0149197020303474) Experimental investigation on flow regimes and transitions of steam-water two-phase flow in narrow rectangular horizontal channels.
- [P] KSME J (BF02984369) Counter-current air-water flow in narrow rectangular channels with offset strip fins, gaps 3.0 & 5.0 mm.

## C (cont.) Pressure drop correlations
- [V] Mishima K, Hibiki T (1996) Some characteristics of air-water two-phase flow in small diameter vertical tubes. IJMF 22(4):703-712. Tubes 1-4 mm; regime, void, slug rise velocity, frictional loss; Chisholm C as function of D (C ~21 toward 0 below ~5 mm; check); drift-flux C0 depends on D. Later: MAD 19% on a microchannel boiling dataset (Brunel MNF2009).
- [V] Kim SM, Mudawar I (2012) Universal approach to predicting two-phase frictional pressure drop for adiabatic and condensing mini/micro-channel flows. IJHMT 55:3246-3261. 7115 points, 36 sources. Companion: saturated flow boiling dP, IJHMT 58 (2013) 718-734; condensation HT IJHMT 56 (2013) 238-250.
- [V] (Zhang W, Hibiki T, Mishima K) (2010) Correlations of two-phase frictional pressure drop and void fraction in mini-channel. IJHMT 53(1-3):453-465. ANN to find dominant parameters; non-dimensional Laplace constant controls Chisholm C and C0. (authors not in snippet but well known)
## D. Mechanistic dP with bubble/interface terms
- [V] Kreutzer MT, Kapteijn F, Moulijn JA, Kleijn CR, Heiszwolf JJ (2005) Inertial and interfacial effects on pressure drop of Taylor flow in capillaries. AIChE J 51:2428-2440. Two sources of dP (slug friction + bubble/interface term), compared with Bretherton.
- [P] Walsh EJ, Muzychka YS, Walsh PA, Egan V, Punch J (2009) Pressure drop in two phase slug/bubble flows in mini scale capillaries. IJMF (UL repository "review and extension of pressure drop models applied to Taylor flow").
- [V] Cantat I, Kern N, Delannay R (2004) Dissipation in foam flowing through narrow channels. EPL 65(5):726-732. Driving pressure ~ velocity^(2/3) (extends single bubble trains); due to Plateau borders sliding over walls; prefactor depends on Plateau-border orientation.
- [P] Hele-Shaw bubble physics: Hele-Shaw viscous pressure + thin-film pressure jump (Bretherton, ~Ca^(2/3)) + in-plane capillary pressure; Meiburg; Burgess & Foster (effective BC); Booth et al. (2024, PRF/RSPA) "The motion and deformation of a bubble in a Hele-Shaw cell" / "non-uniform Hele-Shaw flow"; Keiser et al. (2018) JFM "Dynamics of non-wetting drops confined in a Hele-Shaw cell"; Zhu & Gallaire (2016) pancake droplet; review "Viscous dynamics of drops and bubbles in Hele-Shaw cells: drainage, drag friction, coalescence, and bursting" (ResearchGate 318560519; authors TBD).

## E. Heat transfer models with bubble parameters
